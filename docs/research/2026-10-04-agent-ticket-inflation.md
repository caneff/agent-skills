# Agent ticket inflation: prior art (2026-10-04)

Question: a burn (controller dispatching worker agents) closes tiny tickets about as fast as the system opens them (14 days: 295 opened, 271 closed; last 4 days: 63 opened, 31 closed; 64 open). Sources: unfixed review findings, spec slicing into 5-7 tickets plus a closer, per-friction "prevention" tickets, tickets about the review/burn machinery, weekly retro digest. What does published prior art say?

Method: web search and page fetch (Exa), 2026-10-04. The repo's own numbers above come from the brief and were not re-measured here. "Unconfirmed" marks anything I could not verify on the cited page.

## Synopsis

Nobody in the sources I found measured ticket inflation by coding agents directly. The evidence is adjacent: reviewer-bot noise, human backlog practice, and agent loops that generate their own work. Together it says four things.

1. Findings should be filtered before they exist, not triaged after. Every system with measured results gates at generation time with a severity or confidence threshold plus a per-review cap (Anthropic's code-review plugin drops findings under confidence 80; Atlassian runs a three-stage filter and reaches a 38.7% resolution rate; CodeRabbit and Anthropic's REVIEW.md template cap nits at about 3-5 per review). None of them turns the leftovers into tickets.
2. The strongest human norm is "fix it now, or it never happens", and the file-a-bug fallback is narrow. Google's reviewer guide says cleanup deferred to a later change "usually... never happens", and only allows a filed bug when surrounding problems "can't be addressed right now", assigned to the author. Fowler's "camp site rule" says fix within minutes or the same day.
3. A backlog is a cost, not an asset. Basecamp's Shape Up drops backlogs entirely ("if we don't [bet], we let it go. There's nothing we need to track"). Kanban queue limits apply the same idea. Steve Yegge's Beads, the agent issue tracker closest to this setup, caps in practice at about 200-500 open issues and deletes closed ones.
4. Self-generating loops do not converge on their own. The evidence is thinner and mostly practitioner reports plus one preregistered pilot, but it is consistent: unbounded task creators outrun completion, and an agent grading its own progress accepts almost everything. The bounds people use are hard count ceilings, depth limits, dedupe, and an out-of-band judge.

### Ranked mechanisms that apply here (rank is my judgement; costs are mine)

| # | Mechanism | Evidence | Cost here |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hard cap on tickets opened per burn, with overflow going to one rolled-up "sweep" ticket or being dropped. Applies at the controller, not the worker. | BabyAGI-style guards: total-task ceiling, depth ceiling, dedupe (RunGuard); Anthropic REVIEW.md "at most five Nits, plus N similar items"; CodeRabbit "3-5 items" | Low. A counter in the controller. Risk: real findings dropped when the cap fires. Mitigate by always letting severity-high bypass the cap. |
| 2 | Confidence/severity gate on review findings before they can become any artifact. Only "would break behavior" survives; nits and style are reported in the PR and never filed. | Anthropic claude-code code-review plugin (threshold 80); Atlassian Rovo Dev (3-stage filter, CRR 38.7%); Chowdhury et al. (MSR 2026) 60.2% of closed CRA-only PRs had 0-30% signal | Medium. Needs a defined severity rubric and probably a calibration pass. Adversarial reviewers (Codex) are tuned to produce findings, so expect the gate to drop many. |
| 3 | "Fix in this PR or drop" as the default disposition for same-file findings; "file" is allowed only for out-of-scope problems that need their own design. | Google eng-practices "Cleaning It Up Later"; Fowler "Opportunistic Refactoring" | Low in tokens, but bigger PRs and review rounds. The case of 1 of 49 findings fixed means the in-round rule is not binding yet; make it a gate (finding list must have zero unresolved same-file items) rather than a request. |
| 4 | Do not slice below a floor: one ticket per component or per spec when slices are under some size; a closing ticket only when it has a check no slice has. | Shape Up "appetite" and shaped pitches (work is one bet, not a task list); Yegge's "2 minutes" threshold shows the lower bound agents already use (see Q2) | Low. Loses fine-grained blocking edges and parallelism. You already did this manually (21 into 6). |
| 5 | Intake WIP limit: a cap on open `ready-for-agent` tickets; creation is refused (or goes to a rolled-up bucket) when the cap is hit. | Little's law and Kanban queue-limit sources; Yeret 2026 on sizing Ready queues to what the next replenishment consumes | Low to implement; the number needs tuning. Bounded by how fast a burn drains: at 271 closed per 14 days, about 19 per day. |
| 6 | Expire unactioned tickets with a "stale known issue" state that is searchable, not deleted. | Zimmermann 2021 (stale-known-issue refinement); Yegge `bd cleanup` | Low. Avoids the duplicate-report problem that plain stale bots cause. |
| 7 | Count and cap tickets about the machinery itself, per component, and require a root-cause ticket before another instance. | No direct source. Inference from AutoGPT-style "perfectionism bias" reports and the "progress mirage" paper | Low, but unevidenced; treat as a hypothesis to test. |

## Q1. Agents and reviewers generating too many findings: caps, filters, results

**Review-bot signal rates are low unless filtered.**
- Chowdhury, Banik, Ferdous, Shamim, "From Industry Claims to Empirical Reality: An Empirical Study of Code Review Agents in Pull Requests", MSR 2026 (Rio de Janeiro, 13-14 April 2026), arXiv 2604.03196. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03196v1 . Of 3,109 PRs in the AIDev dataset, CRA-only reviewed PRs merged at 45.20% vs 68.37% for human-only. Of 98 closed CRA-only PRs, 60.2% had a 0-30% signal ratio, and 12 of 13 bots averaged under 60% signal. Caveats: signal is keyword-tiered plus manual coding on a small sample; it measures abandonment correlation, not cause.
- A bot-consolidation analysis in an open repo (rjmurillo/ai-agents, `.agents/analysis/007-ai-reviewer-bot-consolidation.md`, undated, https://github.com/rjmurillo/ai-agents/blob/c07d51d9/.agents/analysis/007-ai-reviewer-bot-consolidation.md) reports per-bot actionability: cursor 28/28, Copilot 17/19, CodeRabbit about 50% (163 comments, 66% trivial/minor on one PR before tuning), Gemini 0/5. The tuning that cut CodeRabbit from 97 comments to a target under 20 per PR used `profile: chill`, path filters and severity-prefixed path instructions. Single-repo, self-reported; the 34%-to-50% signal figures are the author's.
- Tian Pan, "AI Code Review at Scale: When Your Bot Creates More Work Than It Saves", 2026-04-17, https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/17/ai-code-review-at-scale-false-positive-trap . Proposes a "comment action rate" (fraction of comments that cause a code change); under 40% means net noise, tune until above 60%. The thresholds are the author's heuristics, not measured. Unconfirmed: his cited industry benchmark numbers have no primary link on the page.

**Generation-time gates with numbers.**
- Anthropic, `plugins/code-review/README.md`, anthropics/claude-code repo, https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/blob/main/plugins/code-review/README.md (undated). Four parallel review agents; every issue is independently scored 0-100 and anything below 80 is dropped. Explicitly filtered classes: pre-existing issues, pedantic nitpicks, issues a linter will catch, general quality issues not in CLAUDE.md. No measured effect reported on that page.
- Anthropic, "Code Review" docs, https://code.claude.com/docs/en/code-review (undated). Three severities (Important, Nit, Pre-existing). `REVIEW.md` is injected as top-priority instructions and its example says "Report at most five Nits per review. If you found more, say 'plus N similar items'" and "Style, naming, and refactoring suggestions are Nit at most." Findings "don't approve or block your PR." No measured effect given.
- Atlassian, Pasuksmit/Tantithamthavorn et al., "RovoDev Code Reviewer: A Large-Scale Online Evaluation of LLM-based Code Review Automation at Atlassian", arXiv 2601.01129 (ICSE 2026 SEIP per Atlassian's blog). https://arxiv.org/html/2601.01129v1 . Three-stage pipeline (generate, LLM-judge for factual correctness, ModernBERT quality gate trained on whether past comments led to code change). Over 12 months, 1,900-2,000 repos, 54,000+ comments, 2.1 generated comments per PR on average. 38.70% of generated comments led to a code change vs 44.45% for human comments. Median PR cycle time down 30.8%, human-written comments per PR down 35.6%. Note the metric ("code change on the commented lines in the next commit") is Atlassian's own proxy for value.
- Atlassian, "Comment ranker - An ML-based classifier to improve LLM code review quality using Atlassian's proprietary data", Zou, Gupta, Wong, Work Life by Atlassian, 2025-08-25 (mirror URL: https://atlstg.reaktivdev.com/how-we-build/ml-classifier-improving-quality/ . The canonical atlassian.com address was not confirmed). Posting threshold chosen by A/B test; CRR rose to 40-45%; CRR fell from about 40% to about 33% when rollout widened to all repos and recovered after retraining on about 53K comments. Lesson: a gate drifts and needs retraining.
- CodeRabbit config reference, https://docs.coderabbit.ai/reference/configuration : `profile` is `quiet`, `chill` (default) or `assertive` ("more feedback, which may feel nitpicky"). CodeRabbit KB, https://kb.coderabbit.ai/articles/6354480875-yaml-configuration-how-to-reduce-verbosity-and-nitpicks-during-a-code-review : example instructions "Limit review comments to 3-5 items maximum, unless additional blockers exist" and "Group similar issues into a single comment". No measured effect on the page.

**Validating findings before acting on them.**
- Tanay Kedia, "I made CodeRabbit's reviews a third less noisy with an open-source Claude Code skill", DEV Community, 2026-10-03, https://dev.to/tanayk07/i-made-coderabbits-reviews-a-third-less-noisy-with-an-open-source-claude-code-skill-eho . On Code Review Bench (50 PRs), CodeRabbit raised 300 issues, 77 real. A validate-each-claim skill kept 72 of 77 real bugs and removed 76 of 223 noise items (F1 35.2% to 40.4%). Self-reported by the tool's author; run-to-run variance in his own tests was about 5 points. Relevance: a cheap verify-the-finding pass removes about a third of noise, so most noise survives a verification pass and needs a severity gate too.
- Revix, "What 200 Pull Requests Taught Us About AI Code Review", Uri Gonfaus, 2026-07-16, https://www.revix.ai/blog/200-pull-requests-ai-code-review . Vendor post: 216 comments, 151 acted on (70%); confidence scores let teams triage; rules must be retired as well as created. Vendor self-report.

**Dedupe.** Linear, "Using AI to detect similar issues", 2023-11-29, https://linear.app/now/using-ai-to-detect-similar-issues : embedding-based duplicate suggestion at issue-creation time. No measured effect given. Beads' FAQ lists "duplicate merge with dependency consolidation" (https://github.com/steveyegge/beads/blob/main/docs/FAQ.md), no numbers.

**Google comment volume.** Frömmgen et al., "Resolving Code Review Comments with Machine Learning", ICSE-SEIP 2024, https://research.google/pubs/resolving-code-review-comments-with-machine-learning/ : "tens of millions" of comments per year; author active time "grows almost linearly with the number of comments". The cost of each extra comment is real even at Google scale. The same paper reports ML-suggested edits resolve 7.5% of comments in production.

**Not found:** a published measurement of how many *tickets or issues* an AI reviewer generates per PR, or of a cap on issues filed from review findings. The sources above all stop at the comment, not at the follow-up ticket.

## Q2. Human practices against issue-tracker inflation

**Fix now vs file a bug.**
- Google, "Handling pushback in code reviews", eng-practices, https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/pushback.html (undated; fetched in full 2026-10-04). Section "Cleaning It Up Later": "as more time passes after a developer writes the original CL, the less likely this clean up is to happen. In fact, usually unless the developer does the clean up immediately after the present CL, it never happens... it is usually best to insist that the developer clean up their CL now." "If a CL introduces new complexity, it must be cleaned up before submission unless it is an emergency. If the CL exposes surrounding problems and they can't be addressed right now, the developer should file a bug for the cleanup and assign it to themselves so that it doesn't get lost." This is qualitative; no numbers.
- Google, "What to look for in a code review", https://google.github.io/eng-practices/review/reviewer/looking-for.html : a style point not in the guide gets a "Nit:" prefix, "isn't mandatory", and you "Don't block CLs... based only on personal style preferences." Also warns about over-engineering, i.e. building for needs not yet known.
- Google, "The Standard of Code Review", https://github.com/google/eng-practices/blob/master/review/reviewer/standard.md : approve once the change "definitely improves the overall code health... even if the CL isn't perfect"; "there is no such thing as 'perfect' code—there is only better code".
- Google, "Comments", https://github.com/google/eng-practices/blob/master/review/reviewer/comments.md : label severity ("Nit", "Optional", "FYI") so authors can tell what is mandatory; FYI means "I don't expect you to do this in this CL".
- Martin Fowler, "Opportunistic Refactoring", 2011-11-01, https://martinfowler.com/bliki/OpportunisticRefactoring.html : see something unclear, "fix it right there and then - or at least within a few minutes"; make a note and "come back the same day, before you've hit that final point of being done"; "another day often doesn't come". Counterweight: "genuine danger of going down a rabbit hole... decide when to call it a day". Also Fowler, "Workflows of Refactoring", 2014-01-08, https://martinfowler.com/articles/workflowsOfRefactoring/fallback.html : if planned refactoring stories are where most refactoring happens, "that's a bad smell".
- The "boy scout rule" is the same idea as Fowler's "camp site rule"; the Fowler page above uses the camp-site wording. I did not fetch an original Beck/Martin source for the boy-scout phrasing, so the attribution of that phrase is unconfirmed.

**"No ticket under N minutes."**
- Steve Yegge, "Beads Best Practices", Medium, 2025-11-24, https://steve-yegge.medium.com/beads-best-practices-2db636b9760c : "ask your agent to file beads to track any work that will take it longer than about 2 minutes to finish." That is the only explicit duration threshold I found, and it is a floor so low that it would not suppress most of the tiny tickets here. He also says "You sling issues around like candy" ("Introducing Beads", 2025-10-13, https://yegge.ai/essays/introducing-beads-a-coding-agent-memory-system/), so his model optimises capture, not inflation control.
- Searches for a human-team rule of the form "no ticket for under N minutes" in a primary source came back empty: I found only generic advice posts, none citing an organisation's policy. Unconfirmed.

**Backlog size as a cost.**
- Basecamp, Shape Up ch. 7 "Bets, Not Backlogs", https://basecamp.com/shapeup/2.1-chapter-07 : "Backlogs are a big weight we don't need to carry. Dozens and eventually hundreds of tasks pile up that we all know we'll never have time for"; "time spent constantly reviewing, grooming and organizing old ideas prevents everyone from moving forward"; unbet pitches are let go ("nothing we need to track"); important ideas "come back". Author Ryan Singer. No measurements.
- Yegge keeps his active set small by policy: "I run `bd cleanup` every few days on every project. Whenever I get more than about 200 issues I start thinking about cleaning up, and I rarely let it go beyond 500" and gives a mechanical reason: agents read `issues.jsonl` and it fails above about 25k tokens, roughly 500 issues ("Beads Best Practices", above). In "Beads Blows Up" (Medium, 2025-11-06, https://steve-yegge.medium.com/beads-blows-up-a0a61bb889b4) he says finished and future issues "don't really belong in Beads", suggesting deleting old closed ones and keeping the future list in GH Issues. His numbers are one practitioner's operating rules.
- Backlog bankruptcy: Arda Çeki glossary entry, https://ardaceki.com/glossary/backlog-bankruptcy (undated) says a reset "treats a symptom unless intake rules change" and should be followed by an age limit and review rule. Historical case: DefinitelyTyped closed all PRs older than May 2016 in June 2016 (cited in the next item). Monkeynoodle.org, "Declaring Idea Bankruptcy", 2021-10-02, https://monkeynoodle.org/2021/10/02/declaring-idea-bankruptcy/ : auto-resolve anything untouched for six months as "future consideration", via a saved search like `updatedDate <= startofday(-180d)`. A blog's proposal; no measurements.

**Auto-closing stale items.**
- Wessel, Steinmacher, Wiese, Gerosa, "Should I Stale or Should I Close?", BotSE 2019 (765 projects; most changed the config at most three times; bug reports are commonly exempt), listed at https://exa.ai/library/publication/xsnxcql1jdh .
- Follow-up: "Understanding the Helpfulness of Stale Bot for Pull-based Development", ACM TOSEM (preprint May 2023), arXiv 2305.18150, https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18150v1.pdf . Interrupted time series across 20 projects: about 15% more PRs closed in the first month, but about 10% fewer closed and about 24% fewer merged by the end of year one; merged-PR first-review latency down about 21%; fewer active contributors after adoption. So a stale bot cleans up quickly and then depresses throughput. Applies to PRs from external contributors, which is a weaker analogy for agent-made tickets.
- Opposing view: Florens Verschelde, "Don't use stale bots", 2023-02-25, https://fvsch.com/stale-bots : auto-closed issues vanish from default search, so duplicates get re-filed. Théo Zimmermann, "Managing known issues: a refinement of the stale bot approach", 2021-11-02, https://www.theozimmermann.net/2021/11/known-issues/ proposes confirmed issue, then documented known issue, then close when inactive, reopen on activity. For machine-made tickets the "reporter feels insulted" objection does not apply, but the "duplicates get re-filed" objection does.
- Tool default: probot/stale, https://github.com/probot/stale , `daysUntilStale: 60`, `daysUntilClose: 7` in the template.

**WIP limits on a backlog.**
- Standard Kanban practice limits in-progress and "Ready" columns, not the open-ended backlog. Yuval Yeret, "How to Calculate Kanban WIP Limits in the AI Age", 2026-07-22, https://yuvalyeret.com/blog/calculate-kanban-wip-limits-ai-age/ : the funnel/backlog is "not committed WIP; govern it with selection and aging policies"; size Ready queues "to last until the next replenishment opportunity... not to keep everybody and every agent busy forever"; start at one active feature per pod; "an old queue is evidence that inventory existed. It is not evidence that people had capacity to handle it." Only the Ready queue is capped by this source; a cap on total open items is not supported there.
- Dan Vacanti's Little's-law material (ProKanban, "Don't just limit WIP - optimize it", 2021-01-15, https://www.prokanban.org/blog/https-prokanban-org-blog-dont-just-limit-wip-optimize-it) supports "limits only work if work is not allowed to age". Teams with limits so high they created no tension had cycle times that kept lengthening. Anecdotal (the author's own teams).
- Not confirmed: any source I could open that quantifies a backlog-size cap by name. Several SEO-style pages (teachingagile.com, gitscrum) give numbers ("40% throughput", "Backlog: no limit") without a primary source behind them; I did not rely on them.

**One ticket per component or epic batching.** I found no primary source that argues for per-component batching of follow-up tickets specifically. Closest: Shape Up's "shaped" pitches define one unit of work with an appetite rather than a list of tasks (https://basecamp.com/shapeup/shape-up.pdf , chapter 1 summary), and Google's guidance to keep refactors separate from feature changes while allowing "small cleanups such as fixing a local variable name" inside one (as adapted at https://solmaz.io/google-eng-practices-github , Onur Solmaz, 2025-09-29; I read the adaptation, the original is google/eng-practices "small CLs", not fetched). The 21-into-6 consolidation is therefore the team's own invention as far as the sources show.

**Linear.** Linear's changelog (https://linear.app/changelog/2019-06-27-backlog-and-active-issues, 2019-06-28) separates Backlog from Active Issues but sets no cap. Searches for a Linear "method" statement against ticket inflation found nothing usable.

## Q3. Self-improving loops that file tickets about their own process

**Direct evidence that agents filing their own process tickets converges or runs away:** none found. No study, post-mortem or maintainer post I located measures an agent system whose output includes tickets about its own tooling. Treat all of the below as analogies.

**Task-creating loops outrun completion (practitioner reports, low evidence grade).**
- BabyAGI issue #45 "Overshooting by the task creation agent", yoheinakajima/babyagi, 2023-04-05, https://github.com/yoheinakajima/babyagi/issues/45 : a trivial objective was split into a long task list; reporter says the creator "should know when 2 vs 5 or 10 are enough".
- RunGuard, "BabyAGI and AutoGPT Cost Control", 2026-06-25, https://runguard.dev/blog/babyagi-autogpt-recursive-goal-decomposition-cost-control.html : vendor post; claims task queue depth grows about 3x per generation on broad goals and recommends `max_total_tasks=20-30`, `max_depth=3-4`, content-hash dedupe, and a budget cap. The figures are illustrative, not measured; unconfirmed beyond the vendor.
- DeepWiki summary of vectara/awesome-agent-failures, "Infinite Loop Mechanisms and Examples", https://deepwiki.com/vectara/awesome-agent-failures/3.2.1-infinite-loop-mechanisms-and-examples (undated): AutoGPT "perfectionism bias" - the model "consistently found ways to 'improve' any completed work", so completion checks themselves triggered more work. Secondary source summarising GitHub issues (#1994, #2726, #3444); I did not open those issues.
- "BabyAGI: A Technical History", https://babyagi.wiki/ (undated): the original loop "never terminates"; the second version capped tasks at 7 and ended when all tasks were complete - the first architectural fix was a finite task graph with an end state.
- Nachkov, Wang, Van Gool, "LLM Agents Beyond Utility: An Open-Ended Perspective", 2025-10-16, https://exa.ai/library/publication/rkd3xnxnj6p (abstract page): an agent that generates its own tasks is "prone to repetitive task generation".
- Vague completion criteria are the common root across these reports; the proposed fix is concrete, measurable done-conditions.

**An agent grading its own work accepts almost everything.**
- Park and Choi, "When Do Agent Loops Mistake Stagnation for Progress?", arXiv 2607.25152, 2026-07-27, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25152 (fetched in full). Preregistered *pilot*: across 54 cycles a frontier agent claimed improvement every time while 56% had measured delta zero or below; the self-verdict gate became accept-all and eroded the best deployed state by 19%. Even the strongest in-band judge accepted cycles where 44% were real regressions and rejected 38% of real improvements. On a task whose success is checkable from the artifact itself the gap vanished. Conclusion: out-of-band, real-world-grounded evaluation is a structural requirement for open-ended objectives. Small sample, one agent family.
- Anthropic, "Harness design for long-running application development", 2026-03-24, https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps : separate evaluator with hard per-criterion thresholds and a pre-agreed "sprint contract" of what done looks like; the evaluator is worth keeping only where tasks sit at the model's capability edge, and each harness component "encodes an assumption about what the model can't do" so should be re-tested as models improve. Relevant to prevention tickets: re-test whether each added check is still load-bearing.
- Anthropic, `anthropics/cwc-long-running-agents` README, https://github.com/anthropics/cwc-long-running-agents/blob/main/README.md : unattended loops should "Exit when... a cycle makes no changes, or a budget is hit" and ship a kill-switch file; `/goal` uses a separate fast model to check completion.
- Anthropic, "Effective harnesses for long-running agents", 2025-11-26, https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-harnesses-for-long-running-agents : one feature per session; the agent edits only a `passes` field in a JSON feature list; fresh-context sessions with progress file.

**Self-improvement can degrade or amplify noise.**
- "On the Fragility of Self-Improving Agents: Variance, Task Order, and Underspecification", arXiv 2608.18066v2, 2026-09-06, https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18066v2 : stacking a self-improving loop on a noisy evaluation "can further amplify this noise" (run-to-run variance rose in 71% of cases, best-worst gap up to 10 points); under shuffled task order performance fell 4.5% instead of rising 1.5%.
- "MISEVOLVE", ICLR 2026, https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/a24cd16bc361afa78e57d31d34f3d936-Paper-Conference.pdf : self-evolving agents drift into unintended outcomes along model, memory, tool and workflow paths, including degradation after memory accumulation. Qualitative framing; I did not extract numbers.
- "Safety Must Survive Self-Improvement", arXiv 2610.01073, 2026-10-01, https://arxiv.org/html/2610.01073 : stale validation scores kept unsafe programs in 22 of 48 histories; refreshing scores and validated rollback fixed it. Lesson for a tooling-ticket loop: re-validate old fixes against current conditions rather than trusting past green.

**Bounds that are documented.**
- Agent SDK loop caps: `max_turns` and `max_budget_usd`; Anthropic's Managed Agents "outcome" primitive defaults to 3 iterations, max 20 (as summarised by agentpatterns.ai, "Loop Budgeting", https://agentpatterns.ai/loop-engineering/loop-budgeting/ , undated; the underlying Anthropic page was not opened). Iteration caps are portable across model versions; token budgets are not.
- Task creation: hard total ceiling, depth ceiling, dedupe by content, budget ceiling (RunGuard, above).
- Beads keeps closed work out of the live set by deletion/compaction (README lists "Compaction: semantic 'memory decay' summarizes old closed tasks", https://github.com/steveyegge/beads ).
- Beads' discovered-work model files *every* noticed problem with a `discovered-from` link, which is the pattern that produces this repo's inflation; the Beads sources describe the capture benefit and the cleanup cost, but give no rate or ratio data.
